# Code of Conduct

Moebius is a small project kept going by volunteers. We want everyone who uses it, reports a
bug, reviews a pull request or asks a question to have a good time doing it, whatever their
experience, background or identity.

## What we expect

- Be welcoming, especially to people asking their first question or sending their first pull
  request.
- Be kind about code. Review the change, not the person who wrote it, and say what would make
  it better.
- Assume good intent. Text loses tone; ask before you take offence.
- Accept a "no" on an idea gracefully, and give one with a reason.
- When you get something wrong, own it, apologise to the people it affected, and learn from it.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning remarks about anyone's identity, appearance or ability.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Personal attacks, trolling, and deliberately derailing a discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information (an address, an email, a real name they haven't
  shared) without their permission.
- Continuing anything after someone has asked you to stop.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, discussions, code review, commit messages,
and the project's chat or social accounts. It also applies when you represent the project
elsewhere, such as speaking about Moebius at an event.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour is a problem for you or for someone else, email the maintainers at
**testemail@gmail.com**. Only the maintainers read it. Please include what happened, where (links
help), and who was involved. You don't need to be sure it breaks a rule to send it.

If your report is about a maintainer, say so in the subject. That maintainer won't take part in
handling it.

We'll reply within a week to say we've got it. We keep reports confidential and only share
details with the people who need them to act. Nobody will face consequences for reporting in
good faith.

## What happens next

The maintainers decide what to do, based on how much harm was done and whether it has
happened before. In rough order:

1. **A private word.** We explain what was wrong and why. A public apology may be asked for.
2. **A warning.** No contact with the people involved for a while, including in project spaces.
   Breaking this can lead to a ban.
3. **A temporary ban** from all project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for repeated or serious harm, such as harassment or threats.

Maintainers who don't follow or enforce this code in good faith can be removed from the
project by the other maintainers.
